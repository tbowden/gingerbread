Since your diorama is anchored by a gingerbread house, using gingerbread scraps, cookies, or a sugar cone base with royal icing is the absolute best way to match the flavors and structural integrity of your centerpiece.
Royal icing hardens like cement, making it ideal for supporting a 15-centimetre-tall tree that won't melt or warp during display. Here are the three best sweet, gingerbread-friendly ways to build your trees.

Option 1: The Stacked Gingerbread Star Tree (Most Majestic)

This technique looks stunning next to a gingerbread house because it uses the exact same cookie dough and baking profile, creating a cohesive, professional aesthetic.
• The Bake: Use your leftover gingerbread dough to bake star-shaped cookies in graduated sizes (e.g., from 12 cm wide down to 2 cm wide). You will need about 10–12 stars of decreasing sizes to hit a 15 cm height.
• The Assembly: Using thick, green-tinted royal icing as glue, stack the stars from largest to smallest.
• The Branch Slope: Offset the points of each star so they don't line up vertically. Because the points of the cookies naturally taper thinner than the centers, the tips will visually slope slightly downward, beautifully mimicking real pine branches.
• The Finish: Dust the tips with icing sugar for a flawless snow-dusted look.

Option 2: The Double-Cone "Pine Needle" Tree (Fastest & Easiest)

If you don't want to bake a dozen individual stars, use the ice cream cone hack to get your 15 cm height instantly.
• The Base: Take two waffle or sugar cones. Trim the rim of one slightly so it nests snugly over the inverted point of the other, gluing them together with royal icing to hit the 15 cm mark.
• The Branch Slope: Mix your royal icing to a stiff-peak consistency and tint it dark forest green. Fit a piping bag with a leaf tip (like a Wilton 352 or 67). Starting from the bottom row, press the tip against the cone, squeeze, and pull outward and slightly downward, releasing pressure to create a sharp point.
• Why it works: Moving from the bottom to the top creates perfectly layered, overlapping pine branches that hide the cones completely.

Option 3: Edible Cookie Lumberjack Trees (Best Modern Art Look)

For a stylized, architectural look that mimics laser-cut wooden dioramas, you can interlock flat gingerbread pieces.
• The Bake: Cut two identical 15 cm tall triangular tree silhouettes out of gingerbread dough. On one triangle, cut a vertical slit from the bottom center halfway up. On the second triangle, cut a vertical slit from the top center halfway down.
• The Assembly: After baking and cooling, slide the two pieces together at right angles to create a 3D, self-standing cross-shaped tree.
• The Detail: Pipe delicate, downward-sloping white royal icing lines along the edges of the flat branches to look like snow blankets sitting on the limbs.

Pro-Tips for Your Gingerbread Landscape

• Anchor the trees: To stop a top-heavy 15 cm tree from tipping over on your diorama board, pipe a large dollop of thick royal icing onto the baseboard, press the tree down firmly, and prop it up with small glasses or cans for 20 minutes until the icing sets.
• Texture variation: Make 3 or 4 trees at slightly different heights (e.g., one at 15 cm, one at 12 cm, and one at 8 cm). Forests look more natural when the canopy is uneven!
